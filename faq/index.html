Tracker, notes, boxes & resources

What is the CWES study tracker?

It’s a day-by-day plan for the HTB Certified Web Exploitation Specialist (CWES). Every module in the Web Penetration Tester job-role path (20 modules, 279 sections) is split into daily tasks in study order, with practice, review days, and exam prep mixed in. You check tasks off as you go, and the dashboard shows your progress, streak, and pace.

The tracker also includes a Practice Boxes tab, a Resources tab (videos, exam reviews, cheat sheets, tools, and pass tips), a What It Costs calculator, and an About tab explaining the exam format.

This site gives you all four parts of the CWES kit in one place: the study tracker, matching prep notes for Obsidian, Notion, or CherryTree, curated practice boxes, and a standalone resources page.

What are the prep notes?

They’re the notes half of the kit: a ready-made notes setup for CWES in the app you already use. Open the CWES Notes page and pick Obsidian, Notion, or CherryTree. All three contain the same structure:

  • A Methodology section with ten blank, numbered phases that you rename and fill in as your own web testing playbook.
  • A note for every Academy module in the CWES path, in course order, split into one section per Academy lesson.
  • An Exam workspace for exam days: scope & rules, apps, findings, flags, and a 7-day log aligned with the CWES report.
  • Links to tested community cheat sheets for CWES, starter ffuf and GraphQL notes, and reporting references.

For Obsidian, download the zip, unzip it, and choose Open folder as vault (no community plugins needed). For Notion, open the template and click Duplicate to copy it into your workspace. For CherryTree, download the .ctd file and open it with File → Open.

All three work offline once you have your copy (in Notion, mark pages Available offline in the desktop or mobile app). You can browse the Obsidian vault directly on GitHub before downloading.

Once you start writing, your copy is yours. Later updates on this site won’t touch it. Back up the Obsidian folder or .ctd file like any other folder (Git or cloud drive).

What are practice boxes?

It’s a curated list of 38 HTB Labs machines and web challenges picked for CWES prep, grouped from single-bug warm-ups to chained hard boxes. Each box shows which CWES Academy modules it practices, links to walkthroughs (IppSec videos and 0xdf write-ups where available), and notes who recommended it.

You can access them on the Practice Boxes page or inside the tracker’s Practice Boxes tab. On the site, both share the same browser storage, and the tracker’s Export backup includes your solved boxes.

Boxes are optional. Everything tested on the exam is covered inside HTB Academy’s Web Penetration Tester path. Practice boxes help you sharpen black-box enumeration, exploit chaining, and report writing without guided prompts.

Is this made by Hack The Box? Does it cost anything?

No to both. This is a free, independent study aid, not affiliated with or endorsed by Hack The Box. You don’t need an account or sign-up. The HTB content itself (Academy modules, exam vouchers, and HTB Labs subscriptions) is paid and comes directly from HTB.

Which timeline should I pick?

The CWES tracker offers five timelines: 30, 40, 50, 75, or 150 days, built for roughly 50, 38, 30, 20, or 10 hours of study per week. Pick the one that matches the real hours you can consistently dedicate each week.

You can switch timelines at any time without losing progress. Your checked tasks and notes stay attached to the underlying curriculum items and carry over automatically.

Where do the time estimates come from, and how accurate are they?

They come from Hack The Box. Every module in HTB Academy publishes an estimated completion time on the path page, and the tracker uses those official numbers spread across each module’s sections.

Treat them as a baseline minimum. Many learners find modules take longer than HTB’s estimate, especially when taking thorough notes or solving skills assessments without hints. If you regularly run over, switch to a longer timeline so your pace indicator reflects reality.

I fell behind. What now?

Everyone does. The pace strip on the dashboard tells you whether you’re ahead, on pace, or behind based on your start date. If you fall behind, you can:

  • Use the catch-up and review days built into the schedule.
  • Switch to a longer timeline (your checked tasks carry over).
  • Adjust your start date to set a fresh baseline.

The plan exists to keep you moving, not to stress you out.

Where is my progress saved?

Locally in your browser’s localStorage on your device. Nothing is transmitted to any server. That means:

  • Progress on your laptop won’t automatically sync to your phone or another browser.
  • Clearing your browser’s site data erases your saved progress.
  • Private or incognito windows discard progress when closed.

Use Export backup regularly to keep a JSON copy of both your tracker progress and solved practice boxes.

How do backups work? How do I move to another computer?

Click Export backup in the tracker. It downloads a JSON file named cwes-tracker-backup.json containing your start date, timeline, checked tasks, daily notes, and solved practice boxes. On another browser or device, open the tracker, click Import, and select that file.

Export a backup once a week and store it in your notes folder or cloud drive so you never lose progress.

Should I use the website or download the HTML file?

Either works. Opening the tracker on this site is the fastest way to get started. Clicking Download .html saves the tracker as a single self-contained HTML file that runs offline in any browser.

One catch: the hosted site and a local downloaded file store separate progress because browsers isolate storage per origin. Pick one and stick with it, or transfer progress using Export and Import.

The hosted version receives resource additions and fixes automatically. A downloaded copy stays frozen unless you download a fresh copy. Practice boxes work the same way via Download this page.

I already finished some modules. Do I start from day one?

No. Open Already done some modules? in the tracker and tick the CWES modules you’ve already completed. The tracker checks off their study tasks for you. If you’ve already passed CPTS, CJCA, or CDSA, the quick shortcuts inside that dialog tick every shared module in one click.

You can also click any day’s DAY badge to mark that entire day complete at once.

What do “Free”, “VIP+” and “Pro Labs” mean next to a resource or box?

They describe the HTB Labs subscription tier required on hackthebox.com (separate from HTB Academy). Free means any HTB account can play it. VIP+ means the box or challenge is retired and normally requires a VIP+ subscription (though HTB keeps a small rotating pool of retired machines free). Pro Labs require an HTB PRO subscription.

Access status changes as active machines retire. Check HTB’s HTB Labs Subscriptions page for current plan details. None of these are required to pass the CWES.

HTB basics

What’s the difference between HTB Academy and HTB Labs?

HTB Academy (academy.hackthebox.com) is where the CWES course lives. Its modules teach each web vulnerability class step by step with guided labs and questions, and you must finish 100% of the Web Penetration Tester path to unlock the CWES exam.

HTB Labs (app.hackthebox.com) is where standalone machines and web challenges live: unguided targets you enumerate and exploit on your own. It’s billed separately from Academy and is purely optional extra practice.

What are job-role paths, modules and sections?

A module is one focused course on a topic, such as “SQL Injection Fundamentals” or “Server-side Attacks”. Each module is broken into sections, short lessons that often end with hands-on lab questions.

A job-role path is HTB’s ordered curriculum for a certification. For CWES, that’s the Web Penetration Tester path: 20 modules and 279 sections, all of which must be completed before taking the exam.

What’s a skills assessment?

The final section of many Academy modules. Instead of guided steps, you face an unguided web application and have to apply what the module taught on your own. Skills assessments are the closest in-course preparation for the CWES exam, and the tracker schedules review time to redo the toughest ones.

What are cubes, subscriptions and vouchers?

Cubes are Academy’s currency for unlocking modules individually. A subscription unlocks modules up to a given tier every month. The exam requires a separate voucher ($210 for CWES), which is also bundled into certain annual plans.

A CWES voucher includes 2 exam attempts (the free retake must start within 14 days of feedback and requires having submitted a report on the first attempt). See the tracker’s About the Cert tab for full rules.

What’s the cheapest way to get the CWES?

Pay monthly, finish the Web Penetration Tester path consistently, and buy the $210 voucher separately once you’re ready for the exam. Every month you finish earlier saves another billing cycle.

  • Student email? The $10/month Student plan reaches Tier II and covers the entire CWES path: roughly $220 to $260 total (including voucher) depending on pace.
  • Standard monthly: Silver Monthly ($30/month) plus the $210 voucher totals roughly $240 at ~50 hours/week (30 days) up to $360 at ~10 hours/week (150 days).
  • Monthly vs. Annual: Silver Monthly stays cheaper than Silver Annual ($550) unless you expect to study for close to a full year or want the extra bundled CJCA voucher.

These estimates assume finishing on schedule and passing within one voucher. Budget an extra month or two if you plan to spend extra time on retired boxes. Compare options on the CWES cost calculator.

What are machines, boxes and flags?

A machine (or box) is a target system built for legal practice. A flag is a hidden text token proving you exploited a vulnerability or gained access. On the CWES exam, you test multiple web applications and submit flags as you compromise them, alongside a full commercial-grade penetration testing report.

A write-up is a walkthrough of how someone solved a retired box. Always attempt the box on your own first, then check a write-up to see what enumeration steps or chains you missed.

Your setup

What is an “attack box”? Pwnbox or my own VM?

An attack box is the Linux environment you test from, preloaded with web testing tools like Burp Suite, ffuf, sqlmap, and feroxbuster. You have two options:

  • Pwnbox is HTB’s browser-based Parrot Linux desktop, pre-connected to HTB’s lab network. Paid Academy plans include unlimited Pwnbox time, though sessions reset, so keep your notes outside it. See HTB’s Introduction to Pwnbox.
  • Your own virtual machine running Kali Linux or Parrot OS in VirtualBox or VMware Workstation. It keeps your wordlists, Burp projects, scripts, and browser extensions intact across a 7-day exam. See HTB’s Parrot VM guide or Kali’s VirtualBox guide.

Pwnbox is the quickest way to start module one. Most CWES candidates switch to a local VM well before exam week so their proxy setup and wordlists are already dialed in.

How do I connect to the labs with the VPN?

If you use your own VM, open a lab section in HTB Academy, choose VPN connection, and download the .ovpn file. Then run:

sudo openvpn ~/Downloads/academy-regular.ovpn

Leave that terminal open while studying. Once you see Initialization Sequence Completed, you can reach the target web app IP. Avoid running Pwnbox and a local VPN connection simultaneously.

See HTB’s Connecting to Academy VPN and Connection Troubleshooting if you run into issues.

Notes & reports

Why do I need notes if I can just reread the modules?

During the 7-day CWES exam you need working payloads, bypasses, and exact commands in seconds rather than digging through 20 modules. Your notes also feed directly into your exam report. Record the commands, HTTP requests, and screenshots that worked from day one.

Which note-taking app should I choose?

The most popular choices are Obsidian, CherryTree, Notion, and Joplin. The CWES prep notes come ready-made for Obsidian, Notion, and CherryTree, so pick whichever fits your workflow best. TCM Security’s The BEST Notetaking Apps Ranked compares the trade-offs.

Obsidian is the recommended default here: it’s free, works offline, handles code blocks and screenshots cleanly, and stores plain Markdown files you own.

How do I set up Obsidian?

Obsidian is a free Markdown note-taking app where your vault is simply a local folder on disk.

The fastest setup is downloading the CWES Prep Notes zip, unzipping it, and choosing Open folder as vault. If you prefer building a vault from scratch:

  1. Install Obsidian from obsidian.md.
  2. Choose Create new vault and name it CWES.
  3. Create folders for Notes (one note per module), Methodology, Screenshots, and Exam.
  4. In Settings → Files and links, set the default attachment location to Screenshots so pasted images stay organized.
  5. Keep code blocks fenced with triple backticks (```) so payloads and curl commands remain one-click copyable.

You don’t need themes or community plugins to start. Back up the vault folder with Git or a cloud drive.

What is a methodology?

Keep two complementary sets of notes while studying: module notes (following the course) and a methodology (your personal testing playbook organized by what you encounter, not which module taught it). Mid-exam, it answers questions like “I found a file upload endpoint or GraphQL API — what do I test next?”

Each methodology page should include:

  • When to apply the technique and what indicators to look for.
  • Copy-ready commands, wordlists, and payloads with short notes.
  • Filter bypasses, edge cases, and next steps if it hits.

Most HTB Academy modules also include a Cheat Sheet button above the table of contents. After finishing each module, distill the working payloads into your methodology phases so any attack class is one search away on exam day.

What is SysReptor, and why does the report matter so much?

Passing CWES isn’t only about capturing flags. HTB staff manually grade your commercial-grade penetration testing report, and an incomplete or unclear report can fail you even with enough flags.

SysReptor ships an official HTB CWES template that formats findings, evidence, and remediation into a clean PDF. You can self-host it or use SysReptor’s free cloud for HTB students (Hack The Box Reporting docs — export your report when done, as inactive cloud accounts are purged after three months). Read the official HTB CWES sample report before exam week to see the exact depth HTB expects.

For reporting fundamentals, Cyber Ryan’s Report Made EASY video walks through SysReptor setup, and HTB’s Documentation & Reporting module (from the Penetration Tester path) is widely recommended by CWES passers even though it isn’t required to unlock the exam.

Practice once before exam day: solve an easy practice box and write a mock report for it in SysReptor. During the exam, document every confirmed finding as soon as you get it rather than waiting until day seven.
